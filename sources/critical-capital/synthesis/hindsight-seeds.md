# Hindsight seeds: Critical Capital

Cross-episode observations parked here while the notes are written one episode
at a time. Nothing here belongs in a note; notes are single-episode by rule.

This file opens thin on purpose. The archive covers 2026-04 to 2026-09, eleven
conversations, which is not yet enough for most patterns to be real. Seeds below
are marked with how many episodes actually support them.

---

## 1. The same company, two shows, eleven months apart

**Supported by: 2 episodes, across two archives.**

Base Power appears on Catalyst (`2025-10-09`, on raising a billion dollars) and
on Critical Capital (`2026-09-15`, on what it costs to make power cheap). Same
company, two different interviewers, eleven months apart, and by the second
conversation the valuation has moved and the company has gone from 60 to 650
people.

This is the most directly testable thing in the combined archive: a founder's
stated plan in one conversation against his description of the same business
almost a year later, with an independent host asking each time. Worth reading
side by side before writing anything about either.

It also points at a method. Where a company or a person appears in both
archives, the pair is worth more than either note alone, because the two hosts
ask differently. A cross-archive index of repeated guests and companies would be
cheap to build and is probably the highest-value synthesis artifact available.

## 2. The principals-versus-analysts asymmetry between the two shows

**Supported by: the whole archive, structurally.**

Catalyst mostly interviews analysts, researchers and operators, and the host
argues with them. Critical Capital mostly interviews founders and investors
about the category they personally sell into, and the host builds on their
answers rather than testing them.

Both are legitimate. They produce different evidence, and a synthesis layer that
blends them without marking which is which will silently upgrade a founder's
plan into a finding. Any cross-show claim needs to carry its provenance.

The useful version of this is not a caution but a technique: where the two
archives cover the same question, read the Critical Capital episode for what the
people spending the money believe, and the Catalyst episode for whether it
survives contact with someone whose job is to doubt it.

## 3. "The constraint is not capacity, it is how the capacity is allocated"

**Supported by: 2 episodes so far, needs more before it is a thread.**

Two separate guests make structurally the same move. In `2026-06-23` the
argument is that the grid has a hundred gigawatts of latent capacity that cannot
be sold because connections are sized against a worst-case hour. In `2026-09-15`
the argument is that homes are already interconnected, so residential batteries
skip the queue entirely and become the fastest capacity to add.

Both are claims that the binding constraint is an allocation rule rather than a
physical shortage, and that the cheap fix is procedural rather than
infrastructural. If more episodes make the same move it is a genuine thread, and
one that would land well with a non-expert audience, because it inverts the
intuition that the answer to "not enough power" is "build more power."

Check it against the Catalyst archive before writing it up. If Catalyst guests
routinely dispute it, that is the more interesting story.

## 4. Where the scripted monologue is sharper than the interview

**Supported by: 1 clear case, watch for more.**

The host's opening monologue is written, and it is consistently tighter and more
quotable than anything a guest says extemporaneously. In `2026-09-15` it names a
single binding constraint on a customer's bill where the guest describes one
input among several.

Not yet a pattern. But if it recurs, the interesting observation is not that the
host oversimplifies; it is that the most quotable sentence in an episode is
usually the one nobody with domain expertise said. That is a general hazard of
this whole project and would be worth a paragraph in any writeup about method.
