## The question

What does American natural gas at this scale buy, abroad and at home?

## The answer

Abroad, geopolitical leverage. At home, a demand surge makes new gas generation
close to unavoidable, leaving the question of how to future-proof it.

## The argument

Two claims sit behind the title, unequal in size. The export one is about
leverage: he calls it undeniable that the US is a global natural gas
superpower, and cargoes shifted from Asia to Europe fast enough after Russia's
invasion of Ukraine to prove it. The domestic one gets more space. Many
net-zero scenarios from a few years earlier assumed investment in new gas power
was waning; instead the sector met a demand surge utilities cannot build fast
enough to meet. So he treats new gas plants as near-term unavoidable, which
turns the question into a design one. If these units may eventually run almost
no hours, build them where those hours still pay. One of his favorite answers
is distributed gas: gensets more efficient, cleaner and quieter than typical
diesel backup generators, approved to run many more hours outside emergencies,
selling peaking capacity to the grid while keeping resilience value for their
own site, so a tightening carbon limit strands less of them.

## What you need to know first

- **Capacity.** On call to supply power when the grid needs it, paid separately
from energy delivered.

## Details worth keeping

- The charts come from S&P Global via the New York Times, the EIA, and a
colleague's research.
- The announced-plant surge is only in that chart, not in the text.

## Claims worth citing

All figures as stated on 2024-10-29.

- US exports of liquefied natural gas in 2023 held about five times the energy
of all US solar generation, footnoted as 1,270 terawatt-hours against 238.
(Lubershane)
- North American LNG export capacity is on track to double by 2030. (Lubershane)
- Serving tens of gigawatts of new demand in three to five years without new
gas-fired generation, alongside renewables, batteries and other resources, is
practically impossible to imagine. (Lubershane)

## Where it's contested

Nobody argues back. The title drops a hedge the body keeps: fracking is the most
impactful advance "so far", gas the biggest story "for now", with solar and
batteries expected to outrun it. That the surge lasts long enough to justify the
plants is assumed, not defended.
