## The question

Is a battery becoming standard equipment on a home solar system?

## The answer

In three markets yes, or nearly, and in Texas for close to half. Almost nowhere
else. What separates them is not appetite for storage but local tariffs, outage
risk and wholesale price volatility.

## The argument

The chart is Sunrun's, and the share of its rooftop solar customers adding a
battery tripled in a single year. Lubershane's point is that the national
figure averages markets with almost nothing in common. Where economics or
weather make a battery obvious it is close to universal: Hawaii on tariffs and
incentives, Puerto Rico on storm resilience, California now matching the first
on incentives and the second on backup demand. The case he says surprised him
is Texas, which arrives without the usual ingredients. Retail power is cheap
there and no statewide rule obliges utilities to credit exported solar, so
rooftop solar itself has only lately taken off. His reading is that the battery
rather than the panel carries the value: the household buys set-and-forget
backup while retailers and municipal utilities buy a hedge against extreme peak
wholesale prices. He calls the moment a market inflection, then supplies the
counterweight himself: outside those states the attach rate is about 5%.

## What you need to know first

- **Attach rate.** The share of an installer's solar customers who also buy a
battery.
- **Net energy metering.** A utility crediting a rooftop owner for exported
power; Texas has no statewide requirement.

## Details worth keeping

- California's backup demand is attributed to deliberate outages imposed to
limit wildfire risk.
- Base Power, a new retailer, is built on that dual value.
- He closes on Sparkfund, whose services aim to turn distributed resources into
gigawatt-scale capacity for utilities, and Block Energy, selling
neighborhood-scale solar and storage microgrids.

## Claims worth citing

All figures as stated on 2024-09-24, from one installer's own deck.

- Sunrun's storage attach rate tripled year over year. (Sunrun, cited by
Lubershane)
- Basically 100% in Hawaii and Puerto Rico, about 86% in California, 46% in
Texas, about 5% everywhere else. (Sunrun, cited by Lubershane)

## Where it's contested

Nothing is disputed; there is no second voice. The inflection is offered as a
feeling rather than a finding, it rests on one company's numbers, and the
tailwinds he names, cheaper batteries and what he calls the gauntlet facing the
electricity sector, are asserted rather than shown.
