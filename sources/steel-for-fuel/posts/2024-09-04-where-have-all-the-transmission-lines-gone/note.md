## The question

With new US transmission stalled, how much can technology make up the
difference?

## The answer

Some of it, not all. He is increasingly confident the sector must squeeze more
out of existing lines, and sees real traction there. But he stands by an
earlier conclusion: transmission expansion is still, ultimately, more a
collective action problem for governments and citizens than a puzzle for
technologists.

## The argument

The first chart, from Grid Strategies, records the slowdown in US transmission
construction through the 2020s; its numbers sit in the image, so this note
carries his reading rather than the data. He calls it probably the most
concerning trend for the pace of the American energy transition, because
bigger, longer lines have become increasingly critical for unlocking renewable
resources; their deployment should be accelerating, not slowing. Since it is
not, he turns to the lines already standing. Dynamic line rating is his
example: a line's static capacity is set by the most adverse conditions it
might meet in a season, or in some cases ever, while continuous measurement
lets an operator lift that limit when conditions allow. Then the caveat: these
tools, and advanced conductors, run into technical detail and regulatory
thickets, and he warns that people who do not operate grids are easily charmed
by elegant solutions that turn messy in practice. Which is why his closing ask
is political, not technical.

## What you need to know first

- **Dynamic line rating.** Setting a line's capacity from real operating
conditions rather than a fixed worst-case weather rating.
- **Grid enhancing technologies.** Once called smart grid technology: sensing,
monitoring and control that lift existing corridors' capacity.

## Details worth keeping

- The opening chart is Grid Strategies' "Fewer new miles", July 2024.
- He names LineVision, Prisma Photonics and Ampacimon on dynamic line rating,
and TS Conductor on advanced conductors.
- He points to a bipartisan permitting bill from Senators Manchin and Barraso
(his spelling), and suggests US readers might consider calling their
congressperson.

## Claims worth citing

All figures as stated on 2024-09-04.

- In one pilot of 42 sensors on five lines, dynamic line rating raised the
highest-voltage line's average summer capacity by over 25%. (AES and LineVision
study, April 2024, cited by Lubershane)
- Federal Energy Regulatory Commission Order 1977 asserts the agency's backstop
siting authority in a small number of designated National Interest Electric
Transmission Corridors. (Lubershane)

## Where it's contested

Nobody argues back; the hedges are his own. He is only cautiously optimistic
about the recent regulatory activity. He reaffirms rather than revises: the
collective action conclusion he stands by is the assumption the technology half
of the post never tests.
