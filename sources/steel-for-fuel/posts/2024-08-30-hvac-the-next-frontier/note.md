## The question

Where does the next big block of building energy efficiency come from, as the
stock of light bulbs left to swap out for LEDs begins to dwindle?

## The answer

Heating, ventilation and air conditioning. Lubershane argues the equipment
still has large efficiency headroom despite being mass-produced and mature, and
that demand is rising hard on both the heating and cooling sides.

## The argument

For fifteen years, he writes, energy efficiency has effectively meant LED
lighting, which anyone carrying an efficiency target could lean on. That well
begins to run dry as the remaining bulbs get swapped out, and his case is that
heating and cooling take over. Demand is why it matters. Cooling grows because
the world is getting hotter and richer at once and because population growth is
concentrated in places that are already hot, and air conditioning is among the
first goods households in hot climates buy on entering the middle class, a
pattern he treats as close to a natural law. In cooler climates the weight sits
on heating instead, and he argues that decarbonizing that heat affordably needs
more efficient equipment rather than only cleaner supply. The supply side is
the surprise. Heat pumps are already a mass-manufactured commodity in forms
from refrigerators to central air conditioners, which would normally mean a
mature technology with little room left in it. His two examples are the
evidence that it is still there: a dedicated outdoor air system from Transaera,
built on a novel material, and a mini split from Quilt.

## What you need to know first

- **Dedicated outdoor air system.** Building equipment that brings fresh
outside air in and dries it on the way.
- **Metal organic framework.** The class of material the post calls new and
exciting, and what Transaera uses to do the drying.

## Details worth keeping

- Transaera's design point is deliberate ordinariness: roughly the size and
weight of today's commercial units, drop-in, needing no special accommodation
to install.
- Quilt's product is a two-zone mini split, a heat pump serving two spaces. The
company is two years old, and he says its test data does not capture the
sensing and controls shipped with the unit.
- Both magnitudes sit in charts, not prose: an outside lab test for Transaera
and an uncaptioned chart for Quilt. This note cannot carry either number.

## Claims worth citing

All figures as stated on 2024-08-30.

- In the United States roughly 4% of primary energy goes to cooling and 13% to
space and water heating. (Lubershane)
- The International Energy Agency's baseline outlook for global cooling demand
forecasts adding an entire US electricity sector's worth of new demand by
mid-century. (IEA, cited by Lubershane)
- Hundreds of millions of heat pumps are made every year across all form
factors. (Lubershane)
- Quilt's mini split is the most efficient on the market, and Transaera's unit
beats incumbent systems on dehumidification efficiency by a wide margin. Both
are his readings of the portfolio companies' own test results. (Lubershane)

## Where it's contested

Nothing is disputed and there is no second voice. Lubershane twice marks his
position as conviction rather than finding: that HVAC step-changes are among
the biggest levers for smoothing decarbonization, and that HVAC succeeds LED as
the next efficiency frontier. The premise that the LED opportunity is running
out carries the framing and is asserted rather than shown. Both results are
launch-stage tests: Transaera has one customer installation, and no operating
data is reported for either product.
