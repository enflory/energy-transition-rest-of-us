## The question

How much do local restrictions matter, against everything else slowing
renewables down?

## The answer

Less than the macro constraints for now, but rising fast enough to be a serious
concern, and hard to fix.

## The argument

He frames local ordinances as an often overlooked but increasingly important
factor. They are still not as problematic as the macro variables he lists:
interest rates, profitability pressure and transmission congestion. At the rate
they are accumulating, though, he treats them as a serious concern, and an
awkward one, because permitting authority in the United States is split across
national, state and local government, leaving no single lever to pull. His fix
is two-sided: cut federal review rules he thinks add bureaucracy without
preventing much environmental damage, while accepting that projects spanning
thousands of acres will always need permits, which is why permitting software
appeals to him. He closes outward: his sense is that increasingly undeniable
limits on renewables growth are pushing more people towards nuclear.

## What you need to know first

- **BANANA.** His footnoted gloss: "Build Absolutely Nothing Anywhere Near
Anyone."
- **The ADVANCE Act**, which he expands as Accelerating Deployment of Versatile,
Advanced Nuclear for Clean Energy.

## Details worth keeping

- The chart is from "Opposition to Renewable Energy Facilities in the United
States: June 2024 Edition", Columbia Law School, with his firm's analysis of
earlier editions; a second chart is uncaptioned.
- Offshore wind he judges more exposed to NIMBYs and BANANAs than onshore wind
and solar, and says the Vineyard Wind blade failure and the closures that
followed will not help.
- He names the permitting platform Transect approvingly, and files the trend
under his phrase "The Adolescence of Renewables".

## Claims worth citing

All figures as stated on 2024-08-01. The trend is read off a chart.

- The ADVANCE Act is now law. (Lubershane)
- Local restrictions are accumulating; the tally itself is in the figure, not in
the prose. (Columbia Law School, June 2024, with EIP analysis, presented by
Lubershane)

## Where it's contested

Nothing is disputed; there is no second voice. The load-bearing claim, that the
rise is serious, rests entirely on a chart this note cannot see. The link
from permitting to a nuclear revival is marked as his sense, not evidence, and he
endorses both deregulation and a named vendor without testing either.
