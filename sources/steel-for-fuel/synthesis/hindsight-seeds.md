# Hindsight seeds: Steel For Fuel

Cross-post observations parked here while the notes are written one post at a
time. Nothing here belongs in a note; notes are single-item by rule.

This file opens thin on purpose. Two notes exist so far, both written by hand
as references, so nearly everything below is an observation about the corpus
rather than a finding from reading it. Seeds are marked with how many posts
actually support them.

## 1. An author who revises himself in public, with dates

**Supported by: 5 posts, and probably many more once the notes exist.**

This is the thing this source has that the podcast archives structurally
cannot. A guest on Catalyst says what they think in October 2024 and is not
asked about it again. Lubershane returns to his own arguments, quotes his
earlier wording exactly, and says what changed.

Observed so far:

- `2024-11-20` "For AI, energy is nothing, and energy is everything" and
  `2026-09-21` the reprise, which quotes it and complicates it.
- `2026-09-21` also quotes `2026-08-05`, six weeks earlier, on whether a data
  center raises your bill.
- `2026-03-13` "The robots are coming" revisits both `2025-05-28` "An Ode to
  Physical AI", which it names in its opening line, and `2025-07-16`. The
  duplicate checker scores the two pairs at 52% and 28%.

The synthesis opportunity: a chronology of positions held, quoted and revised,
with his own stated reasons for each revision. Nothing else in the archive
supplies that, because nothing else has the same person on the record across
three years with explicit self-citation. Build it from the `> ` quoted blocks,
which the scraper preserves precisely so this is possible.

**The caution.** Do not mistake the quoted passage for the current view; that
is the single most likely error on this source, and it is why notes are written
one post at a time. The chronology is only trustworthy if each note got its own
post right first.

## 2. AI load is the spine of the recent archive

**Supported by: at least 8 posts, 2024-07 onward, by title alone.**

From `2024-07-26` "AI versus computing efficiency" through `2026-09-21`, a
large share of the recent output is one continuing argument about how much
electricity AI will actually draw and what it can afford to pay for it. The
2026 posts on affordability and on the compute heat rate are the most recent
instalments.

Worth tracing as a single thread once notes exist, and worth comparing against
the Catalyst archive's own heavy coverage of the same question, where the
claims are made by guests rather than by one person over time.

## 3. A working investor's disclosure practice, as a corpus

**Supported by: 35 posts mentioning the firm, ~30 with an explicit statement.**

Lubershane names Energy Impact Partners in most posts and states portfolio
relationships inline and unprompted. That is unusual enough to be worth
describing accurately somewhere, and it is a fact about the corpus rather than
about any post, so it does not belong in a note.

The narrow question a synthesis could answer: does the disclosure appear
wherever a portfolio company is discussed, or only sometimes? That is
answerable from the archive and is not answerable from any single post.
